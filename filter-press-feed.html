Filter press feed pumps that hold pressure through the whole cycle

A filter press asks for low flow at first, then constant pressure as the cake builds. A centrifugal pump delivers the opposite. A positive-displacement slurry pump delivers what the press actually needs.

The cycle, and why it matters

Filling: the chambers are empty, so resistance is low and flow is high. Squeeze: the cake is formed, resistance rises, and the pump must hold pressure at very low flow without dead-heading itself to destruction.

A centrifugal pump lives on its curve — as resistance rises, flow collapses and so does pressure. Operators compensate by throttling or by accepting a longer cycle. A plunger pump displaces a fixed volume per stroke, so pressure is set by the relief valve and the cycle stays short.

Practical benefits

  • Shorter cycles because pressure is held to the end of the fill.
  • Drier cake from consistent terminal pressure, which cuts transport and drying cost.
  • No cavitation at the suction — the vertical five-plunger layout keeps the inlet flooded.
  • Predictable wear — ceramic plungers and alloy seats, 4,000 h+.

Sizing note: feed pumps are usually sized on the fill rate, not on the average flow. Tell us chamber volume, cycle time target and slurry density.
