Application · mining

Mine dewatering pumps for high head and abrasive water

Underground sumps to surface, often in stages. The water carries fines, the head is high, and a stopped pump stops the mine.

What goes wrong with centrifugal dewatering pumps

A multistage centrifugal pump is efficient on clean water at its design point. Underground, neither condition holds: the water carries fines that open wear rings, and the head varies with sump level, so the pump runs off its best efficiency point for much of the day.

The result is a pump that starts at 65% and settles near 45–50%, with impeller and wear-ring replacement every few months. In a three-pump-house arrangement, the maintenance burden multiplies.

How we specify it

  • Positive displacement multi-plunger pumps hold efficiency as the head changes — output is set by speed, not by the system curve.
  • Ceramic or hardened plungers handle fines that would erode a centrifugal impeller.
  • Parallel or series staging across pump houses, with each unit sized to its own lift.
  • Level-based speed control so the pump matches inflow instead of cycling.

Reference: three pump houses at −50, −250 and −500 m on a Chinese coal mine, replacing 24 multistage centrifugal units with six multi-plunger machines. Measured energy saving 22–37% across the three stations.

Selection range

Typical dewatering duties fall in the UDZ 160/16 and UDZ 320/16 range: 100–320 m³/h at 16 MPa and above. Where the lift is lower but the flow higher, the V5 slurry pump handles dirty water with solids.
